HOW BILLBOARD TRACERS WORK — explained simply
================================================

1. VERTEX (plural: VERTICES)
-----------------------------
A vertex is just a point in 3D space. Think of it like a pin on a map.
It has a position (x, y, z) — where it is in the world.
It also carries extra info like color and UV (texture coordinates).

Example: a vertex at (3, 5, 0) is a dot floating 3 units right, 5 units up.


2. MESH
--------
A mesh is a shape made of vertices connected together.
Think of it like connect-the-dots but in 3D.

A mesh has two parts:
  - vertices: the dots (positions + colors)
  - indices: which dots to connect to make triangles

Everything in 3D graphics is made of triangles. A square? Two triangles.
A cube? 12 triangles. A character model? Thousands of triangles.


3. INDICES
-----------
Indices tell the GPU "connect vertex 0, vertex 1, and vertex 2 into a triangle."

Example:
  vertices = [A, B, C, D]     (4 points making a square)
  indices  = [0, 1, 2,         (triangle 1: A-B-C)
              0, 2, 3]         (triangle 2: A-C-D)

  That makes a square from 2 triangles:

      A ---- B
      |    / |
      |  /   |
      | /    |
      D ---- C

Why not just list triangles directly? Because vertices are often shared.
A square has 4 corners but 2 triangles — indices let us reuse the corners
instead of duplicating them.


4. QUAD
--------
A quad is just a rectangle (4 corners, 2 triangles).
It's the simplest useful flat shape.

Our old tracer was a quad:
  - 4 vertices making a flat rectangle
  - tail on one end, head on the other
  - constant width from tail to head

Our new tracer is a TRIANGLE (3 vertices):
  - 2 vertices at the tail (left and right, giving it width)
  - 1 vertex at the head (the point/tip)
  - this creates the tapered "comet" / "bullet streak" look

      tail-left ___________  tail-right
                \         /
                 \       /
                  \     /
                   \   /
                    \ /
                   head (point)


5. BILLBOARD
-------------
A billboard is a flat shape that always faces the camera.

Imagine holding a piece of paper in front of someone.
No matter where they move, you rotate the paper to always face them.
That's billboarding.

Why? Because our tracer is a flat triangle with no depth.
If you looked at it from the side, it would be invisible — like looking
at the edge of a sheet of paper.

By always rotating it to face the camera, it always looks solid.

How we do it:
  1. We know the tracer's direction (tail -> head)
  2. We know where the camera is
  3. We compute a "right" vector perpendicular to both:

         camera
           |
           |  (to_cam vector)
           |
     right <----[===tracer direction===]----> right

  The cross product of (tracer direction) x (to camera) gives us "right".
  We offset the tail vertices left and right along this vector.

  As the camera moves, "right" recalculates, so the triangle rotates
  to always face you.


6. DOUBLE-SIDED RENDERING
---------------------------
Triangles in 3D graphics have a "front" and a "back".
The GPU only draws the front by default (called backface culling).

Which side is "front" depends on the order of vertices:
  - 0, 1, 2 = front (counter-clockwise)
  - 2, 1, 0 = back (clockwise, same triangle but reversed)

Since our billboard can be seen from either side, we draw both:
  indices = [0, 1, 2,    <- front face
             2, 1, 0]    <- back face (same triangle, reversed winding)


7. GLOW PASS
--------------
We draw the same triangle twice:
  1. Inner: normal width, full brightness — the visible bullet streak
  2. Outer: 3x wider, 30% opacity — a soft halo around it

This fakes a glow effect without any shaders. Cheap and effective.


8. THE TAPER + FADE
---------------------
The tail vertices have full color and opacity.
The head vertex has 0 opacity (fully transparent).

The GPU automatically blends between vertex colors across the triangle.
So the tracer smoothly fades from bright at the tail to invisible at the tip.

Combined with the taper (wide tail, narrow head), this creates the
"bullet streak" look:

  [BRIGHT =========> fading -------> invisible]
   wide                              point


9. DEGENERATE CASE (the "stationary" bug)
-------------------------------------------
When you shoot straight ahead, the tracer flies directly away from you.
The tracer direction and camera direction are parallel.

Cross product of two parallel vectors = zero.
Zero = no "right" vector = no width = invisible tracer.

Fix: when the cross product is too small, we fall back to using the
world-up vector (0, 1, 0) to compute "right" instead. This means the
tracer might not perfectly billboard, but at least it's visible.


PUTTING IT ALL TOGETHER
========================
Every frame, for each active tracer:
  1. Calculate head and tail positions based on progress
  2. Find the "right" direction (billboard facing camera)
  3. Build 3 vertices: tail-left, tail-right, head-point
  4. Tell the GPU to draw the triangle (both sides)
  5. Draw a second bigger, dimmer triangle for glow
  6. The GPU interpolates colors, creating the smooth fade
